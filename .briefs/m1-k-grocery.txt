## Build

### 1. Grocery list
Merged from all sources: manual adds, low/out suggestions, recipe shortfalls, and
reorder-cadence suggestions. Each line shows **where it came from** — a user should be able to
tell why "2 lb ground beef" is on their list.

- Quantities in **purchase units** via `formatQuantity` ("2 lb", not "907 g").
- **Aisle grouping** by ingredient category — this is what makes the list usable in a store.
- Check-off must work **offline** and feel instant (optimistic update).
- Items the core layer could not merge into a common unit stay **separate and flagged** —
  never silently summed.

### 2. Reorder suggestions
Derived, not hand-curated: *"You usually buy milk every 7 days — last bought 8 days ago."*
The purchase history is already in the ledger. One-tap add.

### 3. End of trip
"Add checked items to pantry" — writes `purchase` txns carrying the `shoppingTripId`, so a
receipt scanned later (M2) reconciles against the trip instead of double-adding. Carry the id
even though reconciliation itself lands in M2.

### 4. Quick-consume items
One-tap tiles for non-recipe eating — a yogurt, an apple, two eggs. Writes a single `quick`
txn via `usePantry().appendTxn(...)`.

**This must be one tap from the home screen, not three** — that is why it is not modelled as a
one-ingredient recipe. Tiles are user-pinnable and auto-suggested by frequency. Support a
quantity stepper for "2 eggs" without adding taps to the common case.

## Non-negotiables

- Check-off is offline-safe and optimistic; a store has bad signal and that is the whole point.
- Undo on quick-consume — one-tap actions get mis-tapped.
- Empty states for both an empty list and no pinned quick items.
- Loading and error states everywhere.
- Status text uses `low`; `low-fill` is bars/dots only (contrast).

## Constraints

TypeScript `strict`, no `any`. Mobile-first, 44px tap targets, safe-area aware.
Large tap targets especially matter here — this screen gets used one-handed, in a store,
holding a basket. No new deps. Create no git commits.
