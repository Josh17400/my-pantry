## Part 1 — Community recipes

Browse, search (by ingredient, tag, time), publish, and fork public recipes.

- Publishing sets `visibility = 'public'`. Forking copies into the user's own book with
  `forkedFrom` set.
- Imported free-text ingredient lines run through `matchIngredient`; anything unresolved keeps
  `unknownAllergens: true`. **Never assume a stranger's recipe is allergen-clear.**
- **Moderation ships with the feature, not after it**: a report flow, publish rate limits, and
  an author profile. A community feature without moderation is a liability the day it works.

---

## Part 2 — URL recipe import

People will paste links constantly. Most recipe sites publish **schema.org `Recipe` JSON-LD**,
so this is a parse, not a scrape.

- Extract JSON-LD (and microdata as fallback); map name, servings, times, ingredients, steps.
- Run ingredient lines through `matchIngredient`; show the user what resolved and what did not
  before saving.
- **Locale trap — this is why it matters:** `parseQuantity` exposes `ambiguousLocale` on units
  that differ between US and Imperial (`pint`, `quart`, `gallon`, `fl oz`, `cup`). A UK recipe's
  "1 pint" is 568 ml against our 473 ml — a silent 20% inventory error. When importing, **detect
  the source locale where possible and prompt when ambiguous.** Do not guess.
- Handle sites with no structured data gracefully: offer manual paste rather than failing.

> ⚖️ Import is **for the user's own book only**. Do not auto-publish imported recipes to the
> community — recipe prose and photos are copyrighted, and republishing someone else's is a
> takedown. Block publishing of imported-with-source recipes, or require the user to rewrite
> the steps first.

## Part 3 — Cost per meal

Receipts capture `unitPrice` on purchase txns; the ledger records consumption. So this is
mostly arithmetic over data we already have.

- Cost of a cooked meal from the actual txns under its `cookEventId`.
- Per-serving cost; recent trend; most expensive recurring ingredients.
- **Be honest about incomplete data.** If some ingredients have no recorded price, say
  "estimated from 6 of 9 ingredients" rather than presenting a confident wrong number. Same
  principle as the provenance layer: never render false precision.
