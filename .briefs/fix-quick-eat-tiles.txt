# BRIEF: Quick-eat tiles are hardcoded demo data pretending to be the user's pantry

## Context

**The Good Pantry** — `C:\Users\joshu\Documents\Larder`. Owner report, real device:

> *"When I go home I hit the plus button and I do a quick eat, it has yogurt, apples, eggs,
> bananas, string cheese, carrots. These are all things I do not have in my pantry, but it says
> I can eat them but I don't have them. And my cucumber isn't showing up."*

This is confirmed, and the cause is not subtle.

`apps/web/src/features/quick/prefs.ts` ships:
- `DEFAULT_PINS` — yogurt, apple, egg
- `SUGGESTED_CATALOG` — banana, string cheese, carrot
- `defaultQuickPrefs().frequency` — **fabricated consumption counts**
  (`yogurt-plain: 12, apple: 8, egg: 15, banana: 6, string-cheese: 4`)

`buildItems()` in `apps/web/src/features/quick/useQuickItems.ts` composes tiles from exactly
those two constants, filtered only by "not already pinned", and **never consults the pantry**.
That is the owner's list, item for item, including the fake frequencies that order it.

Demo fixtures got shipped as live behaviour.

## What quick-eat should actually be

Quick eat is one-tap consumption of things you really have: a yogurt cup, an apple, two eggs.
Its whole value is that the tap is honest — it writes a `quick` txn that decrements real stock.
Offering a tile for food that is not in the pantry produces a negative balance and teaches the
owner the inventory is fiction.

**Derive the tiles from pantry contents.** The pantry store is `usePantryStore` (see
`apps/web/src/state`); `hasActiveRepository()` already distinguishes live from demo mode.

Rules:

1. **A tile may only appear for an ingredient the household actually holds** — stock above the
   empty threshold. Use `DEFAULT_STOCK_EPSILON` from `@larder/core`; do not invent a new
   tolerance, and do not compare against `0` (float residue from unit conversion is exactly the
   bug class we already fixed once — see the comment on `DEFAULT_STOCK_EPSILON` in
   `packages/core/src/pantry/stock.ts` before you write the comparison).
2. **Ranking is real frequency**, from actual quick-consume history, not a seeded table. Ties
   break toward the most recently purchased. An ingredient with no history ranks below one
   with history.
3. **Pins are a user preference, not a catalogue.** A pinned item the household is out of
   should either be hidden or shown visibly out-of-stock and non-consumable — your call, but
   it must never look tappable-and-fine when the stock is zero. State which you chose and why.
4. **Default state for a new install is empty, not fake.** An empty quick screen needs an
   honest empty state ("Quick eat shows things you have on hand — add items to your pantry
   first"), consistent with the empty states already used elsewhere in the app.
5. **Demo mode may keep fixtures**, but only where `hasActiveRepository()` is false, and it
   must be unmistakably demo. Live mode never shows an item the pantry does not hold.
6. Sensible default consume amounts still come from the ingredient's form (one egg, one apple,
   a ~170 g yogurt cup) — keep that, just stop inventing *which* ingredients.

Suggested quantity must not exceed what is on hand. Consuming more than you have is the same
negative-balance failure the pantry picker already guards against.
